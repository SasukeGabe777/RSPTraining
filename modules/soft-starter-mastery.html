📖 FOUNDATION · WHAT IT DOES

A smoother start
for a fixed-speed motor.

A soft starter reduces the electrical and mechanical shock of startup by controlling voltage as the motor accelerates. Some also control stop. What they do not do is run the motor at adjustable speed all day.

▸ LEARN · 02 OF 08
📖 FOUNDATION · ATL VS SOFT START

ATL slams.
Soft starter ramps.

Across-the-line is still the default on many jobs because it is simple and cheap. The trade-off is the motor sees full voltage instantly — and the rest of the system feels it too.

Across-the-Line (ATL)

  • Full voltage applied instantly
  • High inrush current and strong torque shock
  • Cheap and simple when the system can tolerate the hit
  • Common source of startup trips, belt slap, and light dimming

Soft Starter

  • Voltage ramps up over time
  • Lower inrush and smoother mechanical takeoff
  • Great when startup pain matters but speed control does not
  • Still runs the motor at full speed after the ramp is over

💬 Memory Hook

"ATL is a punch. Soft start is a handshake. Same destination — very different way of getting there."

📖 FOUNDATION · SOFT STARTER VS VFD

Smooth start is not
speed control.

This is the sales line that matters most. Soft starters help the motor start and stop better. VFDs help the motor run at different speeds.

Soft Starter Territory

  • Fixed-speed operation after startup
  • Reduce inrush, jerk, and water hammer
  • Simpler, lower-cost answer than a VFD
  • Good when the process only cares about smoother starts and stops

VFD Territory

  • Adjustable speed during normal run
  • Energy savings on variable-torque loads
  • Low-speed control or changing process demands
  • More functionality, more setup, and usually more cost

🔑 Sales Rule

If speed control is not required, a soft starter may be ideal. If the customer needs to run 30%, 60%, 90%, or change speed on command, you are in VFD territory immediately.

📖 FOUNDATION · HOW IT WORKS

SCRs first.
Bypass later.

Soft starters use SCRs to control how much voltage reaches the motor during start. Once the motor is up to speed, most units close a bypass contactor so the SCRs stop carrying run current and the unit runs cooler.

🎚️
SCR Ramp
Startup Stage
SCRs phase-control the voltage so the motor does not see the full hit all at once. That limits current and softens the torque ramp.
🔁
Bypass Contactor
Run Stage
When full speed is reached, a bypass path typically closes. That removes heat and losses from the soft starter during normal running.
🌊
Soft Stop
Selective Use
Some applications benefit on the way down too. Pumps are the classic case because controlled deceleration helps reduce water hammer.

📖 METHODS · BEST APPLICATIONS

Great for start stress.
Wrong for speed problems.

Soft starters shine when the pain is all about how the motor starts or stops. They lose fast when the process needs variable speed or precise low-speed torque.

Ideal Applications

  • Pumps
  • Fans
  • Compressors
  • Light / medium conveyors
  • Mixers, crushers, and saws

Poor Applications

  • Processes requiring speed control
  • Applications needing precise low-speed torque
  • Continuous speed adjustment
  • Positioning or motion-control tasks
  • Jobs where smoother start alone will not solve the process need

💬 Fast Framing

"Soft starters are for smoother fixed-speed starts. VFDs are for living at different speeds."

📖 METHODS · START MODES

Four start methods.
Four different jobs.

The method matters almost as much as the product. Ask what the system is struggling with, then match the start mode to the real pain.

DEFAULT

Voltage Ramp

The general-purpose answer. Gradually increases voltage over time for a smoother, softer start.

WEAK SOURCE

Current Limit

Best when the line or generator cannot tolerate a big current spike. Common on weaker power systems.

HIGH FRICTION

Kick Start

Briefly punches through static friction so sticky or stubborn loads can get moving before the ramp takes over.

PUMPING

Soft Stop

Reduces water hammer and mechanical slam on shutdown by decelerating the load more gently.

Size by amps.
Respect the heat.

The source pack is blunt on this: size by motor FLA, not just HP. Then layer in load inertia, starts per hour, duty cycle, and enclosure heat so you do not underquote the job.

🧮
FLA First
Horsepower gets you in the neighborhood. Full-load amps is what keeps the quote honest.
🔁
Starts per Hour
Frequent starts build more thermal stress. More starts means more heat and sometimes a bigger frame.
🏋️
Load Inertia
Heavy loads may need different ramp settings or stronger current handling to accelerate cleanly.
🧰

Soft starters generate heat. Closed enclosures and hotter environments need attention to ventilation or bypass.

▸ LEARN · 08 OF 08
📖 METHODS · BYPASS & PROTECTION

Bypass, protection,
and long life.

Bypass strategy affects heat. Protection features affect how complete the solution is. These are two of the easiest places to accidentally quote a unit that looks right but behaves wrong in the field.

Internal Bypass

  • Built in and compact
  • Most common for everyday applications
  • Cleaner package, simpler install

External Bypass

  • Common at higher horsepower
  • Less heat inside the starter during run
  • Can support longer life in harder-duty installations
🛡️
Protection Features to Look For
  • Overload protection
  • Phase loss and phase imbalance
  • Underload or stall protection
  • Thermal modeling

🛠️ APPLICATION · ATL PAIN SIGNALS

When ATL is hitting
too hard.

Customers rarely call and say "I need a reduced-voltage starter." They describe startup pain. Your job is to hear the clue and translate it into soft-starter logic.

HEAR THIS
"The lights dim and the generator groans when it starts."
That is a classic soft-starter or current-limit conversation.
HEAR THIS
"The pump slams the piping when it stops."
Soft stop is suddenly part of the quote.
HEAR THIS
"The conveyor jerks so hard it shocks the belt."
Mechanical stress is the problem, not speed control.
HEAR THIS
"The breaker trips right when the motor takes off."
Startup current management belongs in the conversation immediately.

💬 Customer line

"If the machine runs at one speed just fine and the only pain is the way it starts or stops, a soft starter is often the cleaner answer than jumping straight to a VFD."

🛠️ APPLICATION · QUALIFYING QUESTIONS

Six questions to ask
before you quote.

These six questions uncover most of the soft-starter decision in under a minute.

QUESTION 1
What is the load type?
Pump, fan, compressor, conveyor, or mixer each hints at the startup pain and whether soft stop matters.
QUESTION 2
Is speed control required during run?
If yes, stop talking soft starter and move to VFD logic.
QUESTION 3
What is the motor FLA?
Sizing starts here, not with a casual HP guess.
QUESTION 4
How many starts per hour?
Frequent starts raise heat and may push frame size or bypass decisions.
QUESTION 5
Weak utility or generator source?
That makes current-limit logic much more important.
QUESTION 6
Do you need soft stop or special protection features?
Pump shutdown and protection expectations can separate good quotes from field problems.

▸ APPLY · 04 OF 06
💼 SALES LOGIC · SOFT STARTER OR VFD

Know when to stay
soft starter.

A lot of lost margin comes from over-quoting VFDs where a soft starter would do the job — or under-quoting soft starters where the process really needs speed control.

Still Soft Starter Territory

  • Fixed-speed machine
  • Startup trips, inrush, or weak-source pain
  • Mechanical shock, belt slap, or water hammer
  • No demand for energy tuning through speed changes

Now You're in VFD Territory

  • Speed must change during normal operation
  • Low-speed torque matters
  • Process requires frequent adjustment
  • Energy savings depend on varying motor speed

🔑 Fast Rule

If the machine only needs to start smoother and then run at full speed, keep the soft starter conversation alive. If the machine needs to behave differently at different speeds, jump to VFD.

▸ APPLY · 05 OF 06
💼 SALES LOGIC · MISTAKES & SUBSTITUTIONS

Don't make these
expensive mistakes.

The source pack called these out for a reason: they are the exact misses that create callbacks and bad substitutions.

Common Sales Mistakes

  • Thinking soft starter = speed controller
  • Ignoring starts per hour
  • Undersizing off horsepower for cost reasons
  • Missing pump soft-stop needs
  • Forgetting enclosure heat and ventilation

Substitution Rules

  • Higher current rating is acceptable
  • Same voltage class is required
  • Same or better protection features are required
  • Lower current rating is not acceptable
  • Never replace a VFD with a soft starter when speed control is needed
×

Undersizing because the HP looked close

Soft starters are typically rated in amps. If the FLA or starts-per-hour reality is higher than expected, the quote fails even when the horsepower sounded fine.

×

Replacing VFD logic with soft-starter logic

If the application truly needs speed variation, low-speed torque, or process adjustment, the soft starter is the wrong product no matter how attractive the price looks.

💼 SALES LOGIC · DECISION PATH

Run the path.
Close the quote.

These few checks tell you whether to stay ATL, move to soft starter, or escalate to VFD.

1. Does the application need speed control during normal run?
YES → VFD. Stop treating this like a startup-only problem.
NO → Continue. Soft starter is still alive.
2. Is startup causing electrical or mechanical pain?
YES → Soft starter conversation now: inrush, trips, jerk, belt shock, water hammer.
NO → ATL may still be enough if the system tolerates the hit.
3. Weak utility or generator source?
YES → Current limit or more careful soft-starter sizing matters.
NO → Default voltage-ramp logic may be fine.
4. Need soft stop or stronger protection features?
YES → Favor a more complete soft-starter package.
NO → Simpler soft-starter selection may work.
