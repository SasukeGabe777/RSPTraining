📘 HARMONICS BASICS

What harmonics are.

In a perfect electrical system, current is drawn as a smooth sine wave. VFDs interrupt that smooth shape by pulling current in pulses.

Clean Utility Current
Smooth sine wave = normal power flow.
VFD Pulse Draw
Current pulses distort the waveform and create harmonics.
🌀
Definition
Distorted Electrical Waveforms
Harmonics are unwanted waveform distortion caused when nonlinear loads draw current unevenly. In this module, the load causing the distortion is usually a VFD rectifier pulling current in short bursts instead of smoothly.
💡 ONE-LINER: Harmonics are what smooth AC current looks like after a VFD chews it into pulses.

Why drives create them.

The rectifier section inside a VFD converts AC to DC. That front-end rectifier is where the pulsed current draw begins.

Before the VFD = the key clue

Most harmonic filters are installed before the VFD because the problem is mainly created on the incoming power side.

Normal for drives, not harmless

Harmonics are a normal side effect of VFDs. The sales job is not to panic about them — it is to know when the distortion is bad enough to justify mitigation.

📘 HARMONICS BASICS

Why power quality suffers.

Harmonics are not just a drive problem. They stress the whole electrical system and can cause symptoms far away from the VFD itself.

Heat
Transformer overheating from extra RMS current.
Cables
Cable heating and avoidable conductor stress.
Protection
Breaker nuisance tripping and unstable protection behavior.
Generator
Generator instability when distorted current hits a weak source.
Capacitors
Power-factor capacitor overheating and shortened life.
Noise
Electrical noise that leaks into controls and instrumentation.
Electronics
Sensitive equipment malfunctions on a shared service.
Quality
Poor power quality that engineers and utilities will notice fast.

📣 Translation

If a customer says, "The transformer runs hot," "The generator hates these drives," or "The PLC gets weird when the VFD starts," think input-side harmonics before you think motor-side accessories.

⚡ FILTER TYPES · PASSIVE

Passive filters.

The most common industrial harmonic filter. Lower cost, reliable, and purpose-built for cleaning up the incoming line side of a VFD.

🧩
What It Is
Capacitors + Inductors + Tuned Circuits
A passive harmonic filter uses inductors and capacitors arranged to block or absorb targeted harmonic content. In plain English: it shapes the incoming current so the drive pulls cleaner power from the source.

✅ Advantages

  • Lower cost than active filtering
  • Reliable, common industrial solution
  • Excellent fit for dedicated-drive applications

⚠️ Limitations

  • Performance is fixed, not dynamic
  • Less adaptable to rapidly changing loads
  • Still needs correct sizing and application

💬 Customer Version

"This is the standard industrial answer when a drive needs cleaner incoming power. It is dependable and usually the first harmonic-filter style we quote."

⚡ FILTER TYPES · ACTIVE

Active filters.

Active filters monitor the harmonics present and inject opposite current to cancel them — basically noise-canceling headphones for electrical systems.

🎧
Noise-Canceling Logic
Dynamic Harmonic Correction
Instead of relying on fixed inductors and capacitors alone, an active filter senses distortion in real time and electronically pushes back against it. That makes active filters stronger where loads vary a lot or multiple drives share a system.
Filter TypeStrengthTrade-OffBest Fit
PassiveLower cost, reliable, commonFixed performanceDedicated drive applications
ActiveDynamic correction, handles changing loadsHigher cost, more complexMultiple drives, changing load profiles, tougher compliance problems

When Active Makes Sense

Loads change, multiple drives share the same system, or the customer needs a smarter whole-system response instead of a fixed single-drive solution.

What Not to Say

Do not pitch active filters as "always better." Pitch them as more adaptive when the application actually needs that flexibility.

⚡ THD & COMPLIANCE

THD and IEEE-519.

THD is the scorecard for waveform distortion. IEEE-519 is the phrase that tells you the customer cares about harmonic control formally, not casually.

LOWER THD = CLEANER POWERHIGHER THD = ROUGHER POWER
CLEAN
WATCH
ROUGH
COMPLIANCE RISK

THD

Total Harmonic Distortion measures how distorted the waveform has become. Lower THD means cleaner power. Higher THD means more harmonic trouble.

IEEE-519

An industry guideline for harmonic control. If an engineer, consultant, or utility says "IEEE-519," you should immediately think harmonic filter conversation.

🧠 Extra Context

Multi-pulse drives like 12-pulse and 18-pulse designs can also reduce harmonics naturally. They are effective, but often larger and more expensive. Harmonic filters are frequently the more practical retrofit or standard quote path.

💼 SALES LOGIC · MISTAKES & UPSELLS

Don't make these mistakes.

Most bad harmonic-filter calls come from one of two misses: underestimating the problem, or not asking the questions that expose it.

❌ Common Sales Mistakes

  • Confusing reactors with harmonic filters
  • Assuming reactors eliminate harmonics
  • Ignoring generator applications
  • Missing IEEE-519 or utility requirements
  • Not asking about multiple drives
  • Forgetting THD concerns entirely

✅ Upsell Triggers

  • Multiple drives on the same system
  • Large VFDs or generator-fed systems
  • Hospitals, data centers, labs
  • Harmonic complaints or hot transformers
  • Utility or engineer concern about compliance

🔑 Fast Rule

If the customer is only asking for a line reactor but describing generator problems, compliance language, or shared-system symptoms, you owe them a harmonic-filter conversation whether they asked for it or not.

💼 HARMONIC FILTER DECISION PATH

Run the decision path.

Six questions will usually tell you whether you are still in reactor territory or firmly into harmonic-filter territory.

1. Is the system generator-powered?
YES → Harmonic filter discussion now. Generator applications are one of the strongest triggers.
NO → Continue. Clean utility does not remove the possibility, it only lowers urgency.
2. Are there multiple VFDs on the same service?
YES → Shared distortion can stack. Filter logic gets much stronger.
NO → Single-drive system may stay in reactor territory if everything else is clean.
3. Did anyone mention IEEE-519, utility concern, or harmonic compliance?
YES → Move from "help reduce" language to true mitigation language immediately.
NO → Continue to symptom and facility checks.
4. Are there system symptoms like hot transformers, nuisance trips, or capacitor trouble?
YES → The system is already telling you harmonics matter.
NO → Continue. Absence of symptoms helps, but it is not proof.
5. Is the site sensitive — hospital, data center, lab, electronics-heavy?
YES → Clean power matters more. Filter logic rises fast.
NO → Continue to sizing and simplicity.
6. Is it a small, simple, isolated drive with no complaints and no compliance need?
YES → Reactor may be enough. Do not oversell.
NO → Harmonic filter deserves a serious recommendation.
