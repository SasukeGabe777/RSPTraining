📦 LEARN LEVEL · THE BASICS

What IS an enclosure?

A purpose-built box or cabinet designed to isolate electrical and electronic devices from their environment. That's it. That's the whole job.

PROTECTED INSIDE
⚡ Environmental hazards
🔧 Mechanical impacts
🧪 Chemical exposure

What goes inside:

  • ▸Control panels — the brains of every machine
  • ▸PLCs, sensors, instruments — sensitive electronics
  • ▸Radios, communication systems — keep them dry
  • ▸VFDs, drives, breakers — heat-generating, accessible
💡 POWER-UP: Enclosures are one of the most common products RSP sells. Master this and you own a huge chunk of the order book.

📦 WHAT ENCLOSURES DO

Protection. Compliance. Reliability.

Three jobs in one box.

🛡️

PROTECTION

Environmental, mechanical, and chemical hazards. Dust, water, impact, corrosion all kept out.

📋

COMPLIANCE

NEMA, UL, IP the right rating means the panel passes inspection and the customer's insurance is happy.

⚙️

RELIABILITY

Sensitive gear (PLCs, VFDs, radios) inside cheap or wrong enclosures dies early. The right box = years of uptime.

📦 What lives in there

Control panels · sensors · PLCs · radios · VFDs · communication systems · breakers · contactors · terminal blocks · power supplies · operator interfaces · and basically anything else electrical that needs to stay alive.

💡 POWER-UP: When a customer says "I just need a box," they're really asking for protection + compliance + reliability. Treat the conversation that way.

📦 THE THREE CORE DECISIONS

Three questions.
Every enclosure quote.

When helping a customer choose an enclosure, there are three things you'll always want to keep in mind. Application first, part number second.

1

Size & Form Factor   

Always determine height × width × depth, plus:
  • Interior clearance for the gear that's going inside
  • Mounting method (wall, free-standing, panel-mount)
  • Door swing direction and clearance
2

Material Choice 

Select metallic vs. non-metallic based on:
  • Indoor vs. Outdoor environment
  • NEMA Rating requirements
  • UV exposure (sunlight)
  • Impact risk (forklift territory? high-traffic?)
  • Corrosive environments (chemicals, salt, washdown)
  • Cost constraints
3

Rating Requirement 

Determine the correct NEMA / UL Type / IP Rating:
  • What is it being protected from? (water, dust, oil, hazardous gas)
  • Indoor or outdoor?
  • Hazardous location classification (Class/Division)?
💡 POWER-UP: The matrices on the next pages turn these three decisions into clear, confident enclosure recommendations.

📦 NEMA vs IP · THE FINE PRINT

Quick safety rules.

NEMA and IP look interchangeable. They aren't. Here's where they part ways.

📋 NEMA includes:

  • ▸Construction quality
  • ▸Corrosion testing
  • ▸Gasket aging
  • ▸Sealing tests
  • ▸Hazardous location classification

⚠️ IP measures ONLY:

  • ▸Solid object ingress (dust)
  • ▸Liquid ingress (water)
  • ▸Nothing else.

IP does NOT address explosion, ignition, or hazardous locations.

🚨 The non-negotiables

  • !NEMA 7, 8, 9 have NO IP equivalents. If it's hazardous, it's NEMA — full stop.
  • !Hazardous location? Specify NEMA + Class/Division. Never just IP.
  • !Environmental only? IP or NEMA both acceptable.
  • !Never substitute IP for explosion-proof ratings. Ever.

🛠️ APPLY LEVEL · IN PRACTICE

Three decisions, one quote.

Walk through every quote out loud. The customer hears confidence. You hit the right answer in 60 seconds.

📞 Live Example

Customer says: "I need a panel enclosure for an outdoor pump station near the coast. It'll house a contactor, a relay, and a small breaker."

1

Size & Form Factor

Total gear footprint with clearance → ~16" × 12" × 6" wall-mount.
Confirm door-swing direction and sub-panel mounting.
2

Material Choice

Outdoor + saltwater spray = Stainless 316 (or polycarbonate for non-metallic). UV present, impact possible. Painted steel would rust within months.
3

Rating Requirement

Outdoor + corrosion + occasional wash = NEMA 4X (IP66). If submersion is possible during flooding → 6P.
💡 The quote: Hoffman 16×12×6 Stainless 316 NEMA 4X · or Stahlin Polycarbonate 4X equivalent for cost.

🛠️ SUBSTITUTION LOGIC

Swap brands.
Never swap protection.

A safe substitute is the same form factor at the same or higher rating in the same or better material. Downgrade nothing.

①
Match form factor — H × W × D, mounting style, knockouts.
②
Match or upgrade material — 304→316 fine, 316→304 NEVER.
③
Match or upgrade NEMA rating — 4X→6P fine, 4X→3R NEVER.
④
Confirm features — hinges, latches, gland plate, window cutout.

✅ SAFE SWAPS

Hoffman painted steel NEMA 12 → Rittal painted steel NEMA 12
Stahlin polycarbonate 4X → Allied Moulded polycarbonate 4X
Killark NEMA 7 → Adalet NEMA 7

🚫 DEAL-KILLERS

316SS → 304SS "to save money" — corrodes early.
NEMA 4X → NEMA 12 outdoors — leaks.
Killark explosion-proof → Hoffman 4X — code violation.

💡 POWER-UP: When in doubt: same size, same or better material, same or better rating. Confirm with the customer before you click "send quote."

🛠️ THE 5 ENCLOSURE MISTAKES

Mistakes that kill deals.

Every one of these has cost reps a callback or a returned panel. Memorize and avoid.

1

Specifying NEMA 1 for outdoor.

NEMA 1 is indoor general-purpose ONLY. Rain pours straight in. Outdoor needs a minimum of NEMA 3R.

2

Painted steel near saltwater.

The coating fails within 6–18 months. Specify 316 stainless or polycarbonate from day one.

3

ABS outdoors.

UV light cracks ABS in a single summer. Polycarbonate is the outdoor non-metallic answer.

4

Forgetting hazardous classification.

Class I Div 1 needs NEMA 7 (Killark). IP66 is NOT a substitute. Always ask "is this a hazardous location?"

5

Substituting cheaper material to hit budget.

You won the quote, but lost the customer when the panel rusts. Either keep spec or push back early.

💡 POWER-UP: Most callbacks come from mistakes #1 and #2. Catch them in the discovery call, not after install.

🛠️ THE 5-QUESTION SCRIPT

Five questions.
Every enclosure call.

If you ask these in order, the right enclosure quotes itself.

1

Indoor or outdoor?

Drives material and minimum NEMA. Outdoor → NEMA 3R floor, polycarbonate or stainless preferred.
2

What's going inside?

Drives the size. Get the device list and add 25% clearance for terminations and air flow.
3

What's the environment?

Water, dust, chemicals, salt, hazardous gas, washdown? Determines rating + material.
4

How is it mounted?

Wall, floor, panel, pole. Tells you depth, gland-plate location, and door-swing.
5

Brand preference, lead time, budget?

Brand on the spec? Need it in 3 days? Tight budget? These three flag substitutions and stock decisions.
🎯 POWER-UP: Run this script start to finish. By question 5 you've earned trust AND have everything you need to quote without a callback.
